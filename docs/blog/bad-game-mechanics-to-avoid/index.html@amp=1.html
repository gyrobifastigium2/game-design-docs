Game Mechanics to Avoid

  • Gamescrye
    • 3 years ago

    In the dynamic and ever-evolving world of video game development, creating an engaging and player-friendly experience is critical. However, there are certain game mechanics that, if not carefully implemented, can lead to player frustration and detract from the game’s overall appeal. Drawing inspiration from Black Shell Media’s insightful analysis, this blog post explores some of the game mechanics that developers should either avoid or handle with great care.

    Overly Complex Crafting Systems

     

    Understanding the Issue: Crafting systems can add depth and engagement to a game. However, when these systems become excessively complex, they can overwhelm and frustrate players. This complexity can arise from having too many components, convoluted crafting recipes, or when crafting becomes a mandatory and tedious part of the game, rather than a fun and rewarding experience.

    Finding the Right Balance: Game developers should aim for crafting systems that are intuitive and add value to the overall game experience. The focus should be on making crafting a part of the game that players look forward to, not dread. This can be achieved by simplifying the process, providing clear instructions, and ensuring that crafting integrates seamlessly with the game’s narrative and gameplay.

    Unforgiving Stamina Systems

     

    The Core Problem: Stamina systems are designed to add a layer of realism and strategy to games. However, when these systems are too unforgiving – with rapid stamina depletion and slow recovery times – they can disrupt the gameplay flow. This can turn potentially exciting and dynamic action sequences into slow and frustrating experiences.

    Designing a Better System: The key is to design a stamina system that provides a challenge but doesn’t punish the player excessively. Developers should consider stamina recovery rates, the impact of stamina depletion on gameplay, and how it fits into the game’s overall difficulty curve. A well-balanced stamina system should encourage strategic gameplay without becoming a major impediment to player progress.

    Intrusive Microtransactions

     

    Why It’s a Problem: Microtransactions have become a standard monetization practice in many games. However, when these transactions become too intrusive, constantly urging players to spend money, they can significantly disrupt the gaming experience. This is especially problematic when microtransactions provide substantial gameplay advantages, leading to an unbalanced and often unfair gaming environment.

    Approaching Microtransactions Wisely: Developers should integrate microtransactions in a way that feels natural and unobtrusive. These should primarily focus on cosmetic items or optional content that does not affect the game’s balance. It’s crucial to maintain a fair play environment where purchases feel like a choice, not a necessity.

    In conclusion, game development is a complex and nuanced process where every mechanic and feature can significantly impact the player’s experience. By being mindful of these potential pitfalls – overly complex crafting systems, unforgiving stamina systems, and intrusive microtransactions – developers can create more enjoyable, balanced, and player-centric games. Remember, the ultimate goal is to provide an engaging and fun experience that keeps players coming back for more.
